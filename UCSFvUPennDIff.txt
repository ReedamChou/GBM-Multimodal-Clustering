The short answer is yes: UCSF and UPenn are meaningfully shifted cohorts, and that likely explains part of the weaker UPenn validation.

Main Shifts

Age differs moderately before clustering.

UCSF median age: 59.0
UPenn median age: 63.58
SMD: -0.443
Mann-Whitney p=5.99e-11
MGMT is a major shift, both biologically and in missingness.

UCSF methylated rate: 72.60%
UPenn methylated rate: 42.37%
absolute gap: 30.23 percentage points
chi-square p=7.59e-15
UPenn MGMT missingness is also much worse: 57.1% vs 17.0%
IDH is also strongly shifted.

UCSF mutant rate: 20.56%
UPenn mutant rate: 3.11%
absolute gap: 17.45 percentage points
chi-square p=1.22e-17
Lobe distribution is very different.

UCSF dominant lobe mode: frontal
UPenn dominant lobe mode: temporal
chi-square p=5.82e-26
Cramer’s V: 0.335
frontal: 64.47% in UCSF vs 35.02% in UPenn
temporal: 16.17% in UCSF vs 43.37% in UPenn
The core global ratio features are less shifted than the clinical variables.

NC/EN median: 0.367 vs 0.416, SMD 0.059
ED/EN median: 2.979 vs 3.014, SMD 0.109
TBI median: 0.056 vs 0.053, SMD 0.105
So the biggest cohort shift is not really TBI or ED/EN. It is age, biomarker prevalence, lobe distribution, missingness, and endpoint definition.

Missingness and Endpoint

Missingness differs a lot in several imaging features too, especially:

occipital_*_ratio: about 0.289 absolute missingness difference
temporal_*_ratio: about 0.189
global_ed_en_ratio and global_nc_en_ratio: about 0.138
The survival endpoint mismatch is real and important:

UCSF uses OS from initial diagnosis, based on your dataset note in step11_survival_endpoint_definition.csv
UPenn uses Survival_from_surgery_days_UPDATED, which is from surgery
